# WEMA Clinical Action Protocol

Clinician-reviewed physical-only home actions for maternal and newborn emergencies, for a caller at home without medical equipment or medication. This is the same protocol WEMA's generation system is instructed to follow; this document exists so retrieval can cite it directly.

## Postpartum Haemorrhage — Primary (bleeding started within 24 hours of birth)

If she has given birth and bleeding started within 24 hours (primary postpartum haemorrhage): massage the lower belly firmly in circles until it feels hard. Then put the baby to the breast — suckling makes the womb contract and slows bleeding. Empty the bladder. Lie flat and keep warm. Help is being alerted, arrange transport urgently.

## Postpartum Haemorrhage — Secondary (bleeding continuing lightly after 24 hours, or restarted days or weeks after birth)

If bleeding is still continuing lightly for more than 24 hours after birth, or restarted days or weeks after birth (secondary postpartum haemorrhage): do NOT massage the belly. Lie flat, keep warm, get to a facility immediately.

## Retained Placenta

If the placenta has not come out within about an hour of birth: do NOT massage the belly and do NOT pull the cord. Keep lying still, empty the bladder by urinating, get to a facility immediately — do not attempt to remove the placenta at home.

## Bleeding From a Cut or Tear (wound bleeding, not from inside the womb)

Apply firm pressure with the cleanest cloth available. Do not remove it once applied — add more cloth on top if needed. Do NOT massage the belly. Get to facility immediately.

## Gestational Diabetes — High Blood Sugar (confusion, dizziness, or has not eaten)

Sit down, eat something small right now. Get to facility if confusion worsens.

## Gestational Diabetes — Low Blood Sugar

Sit down and eat or drink something sweet right now. After eating, arrange transport.

## Eclampsia or Convulsions (seizure)

Lay her on her left side. Protect her from injury. Do not restrain her. Do not put anything in her mouth. Do not leave her alone.

## Pre-eclampsia — Severe (bad headache, blurred vision, fits starting)

Lie on left side immediately. Rest quietly. Do not leave her alone. Get to facility immediately — this is life-threatening.

## Pre-eclampsia — Mild (headache, swollen feet, no fits)

Lie on left side. Rest quietly. Get to facility urgently.

## Maternal Sepsis During Pregnancy

Lie on left side. Keep warm. Get to facility immediately.

## Maternal Sepsis After Birth (fever, foul discharge, infected wound, or fever with feeling generally unwell)

Lie on left side. Keep warm. Do not touch any wound if there is one. Get to facility immediately.

## Mastitis (painful, red, hard, or swollen breast with fever while breastfeeding, no wound)

Keep breastfeeding or express milk from the affected breast — this helps clear it. Apply a warm cloth before feeding. Get to facility today for treatment.

## Obstructed Labour

Lie on left side. Stop pushing. Breathe through contractions. Get to facility immediately.

## Preterm Labour

Lie on left side. Do not push. Breathe through contractions. Get to facility immediately.

## Severe Anaemia

Lie on left side. Rest completely. Do not exert yourself. Get to facility urgently.

## Sickle Cell Crisis

Lie down and rest. Keep warm. Drink water if conscious. Get to facility immediately.

## Newborn Not Breathing

Dry the baby vigorously with a clean cloth immediately. Rub the back firmly for 30 seconds. Keep the baby warm. If the baby is still not breathing, give 5 gentle puffs covering both the mouth and nose.

## Newborn Convulsions

Turn the baby gently onto their side. Do not restrain the baby. Keep the baby warm. Get to facility immediately.

## Hyperemesis (severe vomiting in pregnancy)

Offer only tiny sips of water. No solid food. No other drinks. No herbal remedies. Lie on side. Get to facility urgently.

## Cord Prolapse (cord or rope visible or hanging outside the vagina)

Get on hands and knees with chest down and hips up immediately — this is the most urgent emergency. Do not push the cord back. Do not stand up. Do not lie flat. Get to facility immediately.

## Placenta Praevia (painless bleeding in pregnancy)

Lie down immediately. Do not get up. Do not press on the belly. Do not examine vaginally. Transport urgently.

## Ectopic Pregnancy (still pregnant, one-sided sharp pain, and/or fainting or collapse, with or without light bleeding)

This takes priority over any bleeding-only classification. Lie down or on left side immediately. Do not get up under any circumstances. Do not press on the belly. Get to hospital now by the fastest possible transport.

## Malaria — Conscious

Lie on left side. Keep warm. Sip water. Get to facility immediately.

## Malaria — Confused or Unconscious

Lie on left side. Do NOT give anything by mouth. Get to facility immediately.

## Miscarriage — Light Bleeding (threatened)

Rest at home. Lie down. Monitor bleeding. Do NOT massage belly.

## Miscarriage — Heavy Bleeding or Tissue Passing

Go to facility immediately.

## Miscarriage — Septic (fever and foul discharge)

Lie on left side. Do not press or touch the belly. Do not touch any wound. Keep warm. Get to facility immediately — this is life-threatening.

## Missed Miscarriage (no heartbeat, no bleeding)

Go to facility now. Do not wait. There is no safe home action.

## Any Heavy Bleeding During Pregnancy

Lie down. Do not press belly. Get to facility immediately.

## Unconscious

Lie on left side. Tilt head back gently. Get to facility immediately.

## Severe Pain (unclassified)

Lie on left side. Do not press belly. Get to facility immediately.

## Baby Not Moving

Lie on left side. Get to facility immediately.

## Unsure or Not Listed

Lie on left side. Keep warm. Get to nearest facility now. Do not delay.

---

Universal rules for all of the above: never prescribe, name, or recommend any medication, supplement, herbal remedy, or drink other than plain water. Never massage the belly for miscarriage or secondary postpartum haemorrhage. Every emergency ends with help being alerted and instruction to get to a health facility, except threatened miscarriage (rest and monitor at home) and missed miscarriage (go now, do not wait).
