WEMA · Maternal emergency voice line

Terms of Use and Privacy Policy

This page explains what happens to your call, what WEMA can and cannot do for you, and who your information is shared with.

What a caller will hear before launch

“Hello, this is WEMA. I am not a doctor, and this call is recorded. I am here to help you — please tell me what is happening.”

Callers reach WEMA from basic phones and cannot read this page during an emergency, so this spoken notice is the intended consent point. This spoken notice is played at the start of every call, before any guidance is given, and is the point at which a caller who stays on the line accepts these terms.

01 What WEMA is — and what it is not

WEMA is an automated voice service that gives guidance on physical actions you can take at home during a maternal emergency, and alerts a nearby health facility.

WEMA is not a doctor, nurse, or midwife, and it is not a substitute for medical care. It cannot examine you, it does not know your medical history, and it will never name, prescribe, or recommend any medicine. It is a research prototype and has not been certified or registered as a medical device.

Always go to a health facility. WEMA is for the minutes before you get there — not instead of getting there.

02 How you agree to this

You agree to these terms by staying on the line after the spoken notice above. You can end the call at any time, and you do not have to answer any question you do not want to answer.

We recognise that someone calling in an emergency is not in a position to weigh this carefully. That is why the notice is short, why it is spoken before any guidance is given, and why we collect as little as possible.

03 What we collect

  • The phone number you call from
  • A recording of what you say, and the text version of it
  • The Nigerian state or town you mention, if you mention one
  • The guidance WEMA gave you
  • The time and length of the call, and a call reference number

We do not ask for your name, address, or any identity number, and you should not give them.

04 Where your call is processed

To turn your speech into text, find the right guidance, and speak back to you, your call is handled by service providers outside Nigeria: Twilio (telephone network), Deepgram (speech to text), Groq (guidance generation), and Microsoft Azure (spoken voice). The service itself runs on servers in Johannesburg, South Africa.

This means information about your health leaves Nigeria. Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, health information is given special protection, and transferring it abroad requires a lawful basis and appropriate safeguards. We rely on the protection of your vital interests during a medical emergency, together with your consent to continue the call after the notice above, and on the data-processing terms offered by each provider.

05 What we share with health facilities

Read this one carefully. When WEMA judges that your situation is an emergency, it sends a text message to up to three nearby health facilities. That message contains your phone number, the location it has for you, a short description of what you said, and a case reference.

This happens automatically and quickly, because a facility that does not know you are coming cannot prepare for you. It also means a stranger at a clinic receives your phone number and knows you are having a pregnancy emergency. If someone else can see your phone or answer your calls, be aware that a facility may call or message you back.

We share nothing with anyone else. We do not sell information, and we do not use it for advertising.

06 How long we keep it

Call recordings and transcripts are kept for the duration of this research project and will be deleted by 31 December 2026. Records of alerts sent to facilities are kept for the same period.

Recordings are used only to check that WEMA is working safely and to correct mistakes it makes. Two recordings from testing are included in project documentation, captured with the testers’ consent. No recording from a member of the public has been used.

07 Your rights

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 you can ask us for a copy of the information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Write to the address in section 10, giving the number you called from and the approximate date and time.

If you are not satisfied with how we respond, you can complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

08 What the call costs you

WEMA currently answers on an international number, so your network charges you for an international call at its normal rate. We know this is a barrier for the people who most need this service. A toll-free Nigerian number requires a partnership with a telecom operator and is not yet in place.

09 What you should know about the current version

WEMA is a research prototype and is not yet operating as a live emergency service. Alerts are currently sent to a controlled test number rather than to real health facilities. Do not rely on WEMA to summon help. If you are in an emergency, call a health facility directly.

  • WEMA understands English and Nigerian Pidgin. It does not yet understand Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo.
  • It can mishear you, especially if you are frightened, in pain, or speaking quickly.
  • It finds a nearby facility from the state you mention. If you do not say where you are, it may send the alert to the wrong place. Say your state or town early in the call.
  • It can be wrong. If its guidance does not match what you are experiencing, trust yourself and get to a facility.
